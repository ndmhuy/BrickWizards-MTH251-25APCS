\subsection{Problem 9. Optimization with cost}
A box with a square base and open top must have a volume of $32\text{ m}^{3}$. The material for the base costs \$10 per square meter, and the material for the sides costs \$6 per square meter.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Let $x$ be the side length of the square base (in meters) and $h$ the height. Express $h$ in terms of $x$ using the volume constraint.
    \item Write the cost $C(x)$ of materials as a function of $x$ only. Simplify your expression.
    \item Find the value of $x$ (and corresponding $h$) that minimizes the cost. Justify that your solution gives a minimum using the first or second derivative test.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Strategy}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item We start by using ($V = x^2h$). We equate this to the given volume ($32\text{ m}^3$) to find the relationship between $x$ and $h$, allowing us to isolate $h$.
    \item We construct the cost function $C(x)$ by summing the cost of the base (Area $\times$ Unit Price) and the cost of the four sides. We then substitute the expression for $h$ from part (a) to make $C(x)$ a function of a single variable.
    \item To minimize the cost, we find the critical points by solving $C'(x) = 0$. Finally, we use the \textbf{Second Derivative Test} ($C''(x)$) to confirm that the critical point corresponds to a local minimum.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection*{Solution}
\textbf{(a)} 
    The volume of a box with a square base $x$ and height $h$ is given by $V = x^2h$.
    Given $V = 32$, we have:
    \[
    x^2h = 32 \implies h = \frac{32}{x^2}
    \]
\textbf{(b)} 
    The box has an open top, so the surface area consists of one square base ($x^2$) and four rectangular sides ($4xh$).
    The total cost $C$ is:
    \[
    C = 10x^2 + 6\times4xh = 10x^2 + 24xh
    \]
    \[
    C(x) = 10x^2 + 24x\times\frac{32}{x^2}\ = 10x^2 + \frac{768}{x}
    \]
\textbf{(c)} 
    To find the minimum cost, we compute the first derivative $C'(x)$:
    \[
    C'(x) = 20x - 768x^{-2} = 20x - \frac{768}{x^2}
    \]
    Set $C'(x) = 0$ to find critical points:
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
        20x - \frac{768}{x^2} &= 0 \\
        20x &= \frac{768}{x^2} (x\ne 0) \\
        20x^3 &= 768 \\
        x^3 &= \frac{768}{20} = 38.4 \\
        x &= \sqrt[3]{38.4} \approx 3.374
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    Calculate the height $h$:
    \[
    h = \frac{32}{x^2} = \frac{32}{(\sqrt[3]{38.4})^2} \approx 2.811 \text{ (m)}
    \]
    
    \textbf{Justification (Second Derivative Test):} \\
    We calculate the second derivative:
    \[
    C''(x) = 20 + 2\times 768x^{-3} = 20 + \frac{1536}{x^3}>0(x>0)(true)
    \]
    \textbf{Conclusion:} The dimensions that minimize the cost are base side $x \approx 3.374$ m and height $h \approx 2.811$ m.
