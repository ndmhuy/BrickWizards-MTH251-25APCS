\subsection{Problem 19. Application: work done by a variable force}
A variable force (in newtons) required to stretch a spring $x$ meters beyond its natural length is given by
\[F(x)=kx+\alpha x^{2},\]
where $k>0$ and $\alpha>0$ are constants.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Using Hooke's law as motivation, explain briefly why a linear term $kx$ appears, and how the additional quadratic term $\alpha x^{2}$ can model nonlinear behavior.
    \item Derive a formula for the work $W$ required to stretch the spring from $x=0$ to $x=a$ meters.
    \item Evaluate $W$ explicitly in terms of $k$, $\alpha$, and $a$.
    \item Suppose $k=40\text{ N/m}$, $\alpha=10\text{ N/m}^{2}$ and $a=0.2\text{ m}$. Compute the numerical value of the work (in joules) and discuss how much extra work is due to the nonlinear term.
\end{enumerate}
\section*{Strategy}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Analyze Force:} Identify the linear component (Hooke's Law) and the nonlinear component ($ \alpha x^2 $).
    \item \textbf{Integrate:} Use the definition of work $W = \int F(x) \, dx$.
    \item \textbf{Evaluate:} Solve the definite integral from $x=0$ to $x=a$.
    \item \textbf{Compute:} Substitute the given numerical values using explicit multiplication to find the total work and the specific contribution of the nonlinear term.
\end{enumerate}
\section*{Solution}
\subsection*{(a) Physical Interpretation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$kx$ (Linear term):} Represents \textbf{Hooke's Law}, describing an ideal elastic spring where force is proportional to displacement.
    \item \textbf{$\alpha x^2$ (Nonlinear term):} Models a ``stiffening'' spring. Since $\alpha > 0$, the force increases faster than linearly as $x$ increases, meaning the spring becomes harder to stretch at larger extensions.
\end{itemize}
\subsection*{(b) Formula for Work}
Work is the integral of force over distance:
\[ 
W = \int_{0}^{a} (kx + \alpha x^2) \, dx 
\]
\subsection*{(c) Evaluation}
Computing the integral:
\[ 
W = \left. \left( \frac{1}{2}kx^2 + \frac{1}{3}\alpha x^3 \right) \right|_{0}^{a}\]
Substituting the limits:
\[ 
W = \frac{1}{2}ka^2 + \frac{1}{3}\alpha a^3 
\]
\subsection*{(d) Calculation}
Given $k = 40$, $\alpha = 10$, and $a = 0.2$.
\textbf{Linear contribution:}
\[ 
W_{lin} = \frac{1}{2} \times 40 \times (0.2)^2 = 20 \times 0.04 = \mathbf{0.8 \, J} 
\]
\textbf{Nonlinear contribution (Extra work):}
\[ 
W_{extra} = \frac{1}{3} \times 10 \times (0.2)^3 = \frac{1}{3} \times 10 \times 0.008 = \frac{0.08}{3} \approx \mathbf{0.027 \, J} 
\]
\textbf{Total Work:}
\[ 
W \approx 0.8 + 0.027 = \mathbf{0.827 \, J} 
\]
\textbf{Conclusion:} The extra work due to the nonlinear term is approximately \textbf{0.027 J}.
